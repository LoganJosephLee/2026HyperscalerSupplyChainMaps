This map is built from one source: filings on SEC EDGAR. Every edge is backed by a sentence in a filing, and the sentence is shown to you when you click the edge. That rule is what the project is for — and it produces specific, knowable gaps. They are listed here rather than buried, because the way this map fails is somebody treating it as complete.

An absent edge means nobody disclosed the relationship. It does not mean the relationship does not exist. Most commercial relationships never appear in a filing at all. Read this map as a floor on what is disclosed, never as a picture of what is real.

Two kinds of company that never file anything

A company enters this graph only if it appears in a retrievable EDGAR filing sentence. There are no placeholder nodes and no greyed-out entries for companies we merely know exist. But "cited in a filing" and "files with the SEC" are different tests, and the difference decides two very different outcomes.

Named in someone else's filing — these are in the graph

Microsoft's 10-K names OpenAI. OpenAI is private and files nothing, so SEC's registry has no identifier for it — but the sentence is citable, which is the test this project actually applies. It appears as a node with no CIK, drawn in a distinct colour, and its entry says so. Anthropic, xAI, SpaceX and Databricks fall in the same class.

Nothing these companies say can ever corroborate or contradict an edge. They are in the map entirely on the word of whoever named them. A public company's disclosures can be cross-checked against its own later filings; a private company's cannot, because there are none.

Excluding them while including Micron — a US filer — makes Micron look like a far larger share of AI memory supply than it is. An overstated Micron with this caveat attached is defensible; an invented Samsung node would not be.

The 10% disclosure threshold

Most of the quantified numbers in this dataset exist because of a disclosure rule, not because companies chose to be forthcoming. US registrants must disclose customers that account for more than 10% of revenue. The consequences for this map:

  • A relationship worth 9% of a supplier's revenue is usually invisible. The threshold is a cliff, not a gradient.
  • Disclosure is normally anonymous — “one customer accounted for 19% of revenue” rather than naming that customer. Those sentences describe real concentration and support no edge, so they are not in the graph.
  • Edges with no thickness are not small relationships. They are relationships where no percentage was disclosed, which is the overwhelming majority.
  • The percentages do not share a denominator. One filing gives a share of the supplier's revenue, another a share of the buyer's purchases, another a share of the wafers it buys. Every number is shown with the denominator the filing gave it, and a thick edge means “a large disclosed dependency” — not a quantity you can compare against the thickness of a different edge.

Disclosure runs the opposite way from the seed set

The six seed companies — Microsoft, Alphabet, Amazon, Meta, Oracle, CoreWeave — are the demand side of the AI buildout. But naming obligations mostly fall on suppliers: a registrant must disclose customers accounting for more than 10% of revenue, so a chipmaker names its large cloud customers, while the cloud provider's own 10-K says only that it depends on “a limited number of suppliers.”

This is not a theoretical concern. Microsoft's entire risk factors section — over 81,000 characters — yielded exactly one supply relationship, and that one states no direction. Supplier-side filings are therefore read as well, which is where the named and quantified disclosures actually live. Reading a company's filings asserts nothing about it: an edge still requires a sentence.

Which filings are read is a choice, and it shapes the map. Companies whose filings nobody reads here can only appear if someone else names them. The list of filers read is in config.py and changes as the project grows.

How an edge gets here

  1. The filing is fetched from EDGAR and cached.
  2. It is split into sections; extraction reads Business, Risk Factors, and the concentration passages in the financial statement notes.
  3. A language model proposes relationships, each with a sentence it claims is verbatim.
  4. Every claimed sentence is string-matched back into the filing. Anything that does not appear is discarded, not repaired. A near-miss counts as a failure: a paraphrase is exactly what a fabricated claim looks like.
  5. Company names are matched against SEC's CIK registry. Anything below a high confidence threshold goes to a human, whose decisions are recorded in a version-controlled alias file.

Step 4 catches fabrication. It does not catch a model that read a real sentence and drew the wrong conclusion from it — a real sentence attached to a wrong relationship passes verification. The relationship_type of unclear exists so that filings which state a relationship without stating its direction are not forced into a precision they do not have.

Other things worth knowing

  • Foreign private issuers file 20-F, not 10-K. TSMC is on EDGAR and is eligible, but discloses very little about named customers — so TSMC edges will mostly come from the buyer side, where a US filer names it as a foundry dependency.
  • Filings are periodic. A relationship disclosed in an annual report may have ended the day after it was filed. Every edge carries its filing date; treat old dates accordingly.
  • Names that could not be resolved are dropped, and counted above. If a filing names a supplier we cannot match to a CIK with confidence, the evidence is excluded rather than attached to a guess.
  • Refreshes are manual. There is no scheduled job. The date stamp on every page is the newest filing in the dataset, not today's date.

Source and licence

SEC filings are US government works in the public domain, so the underlying text can be republished, linked, and rebuilt by anyone. No commercial supply chain database is used as a source. The full dataset is downloadable as CSV and JSON, and the code that produced it is public.
